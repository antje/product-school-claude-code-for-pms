## 1. This is two separate problems wearing one ticket theme

**Hypothesis:** "Phone never goes off" isn't one bug, it's two, hitting two different groups of responders.

- **Group A, genuinely starved** (Farlight, Meteor Mite, The Undertow, Vesper): offer volume in `callout-history.csv` collapsed toward zero in step with the routing reweight. This is a ranking problem.
- **Group B, high-volume, not starved** (Nightwell, Stormwrack, Sgt. Falkirk, and partly Ironvale): offer volume is flat or climbing to among the *highest* in the roster (Nightwell: 21/week by late Aug), yet they're filing the identical complaint. This looks like a delivery problem: the timing lines up with the 4.2 fix for "duplicate push notification on re-offer," and this group is exactly who'd be offered often and close enough together to trip an over-eager de-dup.

**What kills or confirms it:** Pull actual push-delivery receipts (not server-side "sent" logs) for Nightwell and Stormwrack around their highest-volume days in late August, and ask whoever shipped the duplicate-push fix whether it suppresses by device ID or by a time window. Push fired but app didn't surface it: client bug, different fix. Push never fired for offers logged as sent: de-dup theory holds, and it's a fast, isolated fix.

**Decision needed:** who owns pulling delivery logs, and by when; this shouldn't leave the room as "worth looking into."

## 2. Specialists may be losing to closer generalists, and no metric would catch it

**Hypothesis:** Capability match is 15% of score and fractional; proximity is now 60%. A close responder with a *partial* match can outscore a distant specialist with a full match. "Coverage gap" only fires when *nobody* matches; it would never flag a qualified specialist getting passed over in favor of a closer partial match.

**Evidence:** The Undertow (aquatic) and Farlight (crowd-management) are both in the confirmed-collapse group, and their handlers independently note there was no shortage of matching incidents during the dry spell.

**What kills or confirms it:** For The Undertow and Farlight specifically, pull the incidents in their region during the dry spell that required their tag, and check who actually got offered those incidents. If the winner usually had a lower capability match but higher proximity, the hypothesis holds. If the winner also had the tag and was simply closer, it's an ordinary proximity loss, not a design gap.

**Decision needed:** if confirmed, does this need a capability-match floor (a responder below some threshold can't outrank a full match regardless of proximity)? That's a call for Wen and Marcus to make explicitly, not leave implicit in the weights.

## 3. The decline-vs-timeout question already has an answer: the gap is that nobody who needed it knew it existed

**Hypothesis check, not a hypothesis:** Marcus's Slack question from 14 Aug (does the reweight treat habitual decliners differently) is already answered in `history.py`: a timeout scores identically to an active decline, a deliberate choice with a dated 2019 comment from Wen weighing the tradeoff.

The actual problem isn't a missing decision. It's that a 7-year-old call was made when timeouts were rarer (90s) and history carried more weight (0.40) in the score, and it's never been revisited even though both of those conditions changed in the same release.

**What resolves it:** ask Wen directly whether equal treatment of decline/timeout was reconsidered when the timeout dropped to 60s and the history weight dropped to 0.25. This isn't a data pull; it's a decision the team can make in the room.

**Decision needed:** affirm decline == timeout as still correct at the new values, or split them into separate signals. Either way, write down the "who gets pinged" rationale Priya flagged as never done; this question keeps resurfacing because it lives nowhere.
