## What we already know, and will not reopen

Four responders stopped being asked after 12 August: Farlight, Meteor Mite,
The Undertow and Vesper, down from 11 to 14 offers a week to 0 or 1. Total
offers barely moved, 4% below the pre-release average. The work went to
fewer people.

The mechanism is in the routing code. A miss costs 0.12 of the
recent-acceptance score and an accept earns 0.08, so a responder has to
accept 60% of offers just to hold level. A timeout counts as a decline. There
is no decay, and the floor is zero. In release week the whole fleet accepted
54%, under that break-even, because the timeout had just dropped from 90
seconds to 60. Most recovered. These four fell to the floor, and from there
it takes thirteen straight accepts to climb back, at roughly one offer a week.

Marcus's question from 14 August is answered by this: the change applied to
everyone the same way. The score treats a timeout exactly like a decline.

Evidence and working: `4.2-investigation.md`, `03-rewind/prompts.md`,
`04-x-ray-vision/prompts.md`.

## 1. Does the score survive a deploy?

`history.py` keeps every score in memory and never writes it anywhere. If
production does the same, the 12 August deploy reset all sixteen to the
midpoint rather than leaving them at the ceiling their summer had earned.
Modelled that way, the ranking alone picks out exactly these four in 95% of
orderings of release-week offers. Modelled from the ceiling, 26%.

**Test:** Wen says where the score is stored and what happened to it on 12
August. A yes or no.

**Unlocks:** everything in the Module 5 brief. An alert on a threshold, a
lift, and decay all assume the number lasts longer than a deploy.

## 2. Are specialists losing to closer responders who cannot do the job?

Capability is 15% of the score and proximity 60%. A responder with none of
the needed tags and an average record now outranks a fully qualified,
perfect-record responder 46 minutes away if they are within 24 minutes. Under
the old weights it was 10. The Undertow is aquatic and Farlight
crowd-management, and both handlers say there was no shortage of matching
incidents.

**Test:** for their incidents during the quiet weeks, who was asked first,
and did that person hold the tag?

**Unlocks:** whether capability should gate the list rather than weigh on
it. Wen and Marcus, explicitly, rather than left inside the weights.

## 3. Why do eight busy responders report silence?

Nightwell, Stormwrack, Sgt. Falkirk and five others filed tickets describing
dry spells in weeks the file shows them at record volume. Nightwell accepted
fourteen callouts in the week she said she had heard nothing for ten days.
No reading of the file makes both sources true. Either the file is not what
we think it is, or the tickets are describing something the file cannot see.

**Test:** Ravi confirms what `callout-history.csv` is, where it came from,
and what `pings_taken` counts.

**Unlocks:** whether we can trust any number in this investigation. The four
starved responders are corroborated by the code and by their handlers.
Nothing corroborates the record highs.

## 4. Did about a hundred callouts go unanswered?

Weekly accepted callouts fell from 132 before the release to 96, 104, 108
and 120 afterwards, while offers went out at the usual rate. If an accepted
offer is a filled callout, that is about a hundred incidents across four
weeks that nobody took. Nobody at Rook has mentioned one.

**Test:** Ravi's count of callouts created each week since June.

**Unlocks:** whether this stops being a fairness problem and becomes a safety
one. It is the question I would least like to be wrong about.
